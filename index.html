Abundance for All

What if nobody ever had to go without?

We believe we should build superintelligence and robotics to provide every need, and every reasonable want, for every person on Earth. Not to replace people. To free them.

Three ingredients

Every need comes down to work, energy and knowing how.

Food, a home, medicine, clean water: behind each one are people's work, power to run it, and the know-how to do it well. We believe three things, together, could supply all of it, for everyone.

01 · Work

Robotics brings the work.

Robots that plant, build, haul and repair could lift the heaviest and dullest labour off every back. We believe that frees people rather than replaces them: the work is the part nobody should have to do in order to eat.

The world's factories now run a record 5 million industrial robots, up 9% in 2025.Source: International Federation of Robotics (press release, Sep 24, 2026) · 2025

02 · Energy

Clean energy powers all of it.

Machines and minds both run on electricity. Abundant, clean power is what makes everything else possible, and it has to be there for every household, not just some.

Solar produced 8.7% of the world's electricity in 2025.Source: Ember, via Our World in Data · 2025

03 · Knowing how

Superintelligence brings the knowing how.

The best doctor's judgement, the best engineer's design, the best teacher's patience: knowledge that is scarce today. We believe superintelligence, built carefully and aimed at everyone, could put it within reach of anyone, anywhere, in their own language.

An AI system's predicted protein structures are now free to every scientist: over 200 million of them.Source: EMBL-EBI AlphaFold Protein Structure Database · 2026

Every human need

This is where the gaps are today.

Real, sourced numbers for the world as a whole, not predictions. They are the distance between the world we have and the one we believe is worth building.

Food

8.5%of people undernourished

8.5% of the world's people were undernourished in 2023.

We believe robots that plant, tend and harvest, guided by a superintelligence that learns every field, could help every harvest reach every table.

Source: World Bank WDI (FAO (SDG 2.1.1)) · 2023 · CC BY 4.0

Water

73.7%of people used safely managed drinking water

73.7% of people used safely managed drinking water in 2024. About one person in four did not.

We believe tireless machines and smarter engineering could build, repair and watch over the pipes, pumps and treatment plants that safe water depends on.

Source: World Bank WDI (WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme) · 2024 · CC BY 4.0

Shelter

23.2%of city dwellers lived in slums

23.2% of the world's urban population lived in slums in 2022.

We believe robot builders and AI design could make a safe, good home something that can be built quickly, to a standard, for anyone.

Source: World Bank WDI (UN-Habitat Urban Indicators Database) · 2022 · CC BY 4.0

Health

37.4deaths of children under five, per 1,000 live births

The world's under-5 mortality rate was 37.4 deaths per 1,000 live births in 2024.

We believe AI could bring the knowing how of the best doctor to every clinic, and speed up the medicines and vaccines that protect children.

Source: World Bank WDI (UN Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation) · 2024 · CC BY 4.0

Energy

91.9%of people had access to electricity

91.9% of people had access to electricity in 2024. The rest, about 8%, did not.

We believe robots could build and maintain clean power at a scale people alone cannot, and AI could run the grids that carry it.

Source: World Bank WDI (SDG 7.1.1 Electrification Dataset (Tracking SDG7)) · 2024 · CC BY 4.0

Learning

48.3%of 10-year-olds below minimum reading level

48.3% of children at the end of primary-school age were below minimum reading proficiency in 2019 (learning poverty).

We believe a patient AI tutor in every child's own language could be there for every learner, wherever the nearest school is.

Source: World Bank WDI (World Bank / UNESCO Institute for Statistics (learning poverty)) · 2019 · CC BY 4.0

How far we've come

From a summer workshop to a Nobel Prize.

A few real moments in AI and robotics. Each one links to its source.

  1. 1956

    The field gets its name

    A summer workshop at Dartmouth, widely seen as the founding event of artificial intelligence as a field.

    Wikipedia: Dartmouth workshop

  2. 1961

    The first industrial robot goes to work

    Unimate takes a job on a General Motors assembly line in Ewing Township, New Jersey.

    Wikipedia: Unimate

  3. 1997

    A computer beats the world chess champion

    IBM's Deep Blue defeats Garry Kasparov in a six-game rematch.

    Wikipedia: Deep Blue

  4. 2011

    A machine wins at Jeopardy!

    IBM's Watson beats champions Brad Rutter and Ken Jennings at the quiz show.

    Wikipedia: Watson

  5. 2012

    Deep learning takes off

    AlexNet wins the ImageNet image-recognition contest with a top-5 error rate of 15.3%.

    Wikipedia: AlexNet

  6. 2016

    AlphaGo beats a Go champion

    DeepMind's AlphaGo wins four of five games against Lee Sedol in Seoul.

    Wikipedia: AlphaGo versus Lee Sedol

  7. 2017

    The transformer

    The paper "Attention Is All You Need" introduces the architecture behind today's large language models.

    Wikipedia: Attention Is All You Need

  8. 2020

    AlphaFold 2 cracks protein folding

    At the CASP14 competition, AlphaFold 2 predicts protein structures with far higher accuracy than any other entry.

    Wikipedia: AlphaFold

  9. 2022

    ChatGPT reaches the public

    OpenAI releases ChatGPT on November 30, 2022.

    Wikipedia: ChatGPT

  10. 2024

    A Nobel Prize for predicting protein structures

    The Nobel Prize in Chemistry goes in part to Demis Hassabis and John Jumper for an AI model that solved a 50-year-old problem.

    NobelPrize.org press release

  11. 2025

    Five million industrial robots at work

    The International Federation of Robotics reports a record 5 million industrial robots operating in the world's factories.

    International Federation of Robotics (press release, Sep 24, 2026)

A timeline is not a forecast. We believe the hard, important part is still ahead of us.
